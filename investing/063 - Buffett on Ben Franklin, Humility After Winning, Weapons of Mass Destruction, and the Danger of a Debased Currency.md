# Buffett on Ben Franklin, Humility After Winning, Weapons of Mass Destruction, and the Danger of a Debased Currency

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1dbhz-CcK_s

Recorded: most likely the 2025 Berkshire Hathaway annual meeting (moderate confidence; 2024 is possible)

> Note: The original YouTube title is "Warren Buffett: Why Benjamin Franklin Was The Greatest Investor Ever". It is misleading: Buffett never calls Franklin an investor, let alone the greatest one. He admires Franklin as a remarkable all-round thinker, mentions Franklin's will (money left to Philadelphia and Boston as an example of compounding), and then moves on to America's place in the world, lecturing other countries, nuclear proliferation, and the need for a currency that does not get debased. Only Buffett speaks; the question (apparently about sitting under a tree with Ben Franklin) is not included. The clip is most likely from the **2025** Berkshire Hathaway annual meeting (moderate confidence; 2024 is possible). The clues: Buffett speaks of "eight billion people" (world population passed 8 billion in late 2022); Munger (died November 2023) is not asked to comment; and the themes of not humiliating the "losers" of the world and of currency debasement match Buffett's remarks on trade and fiscal policy at the 2025 meeting.

## Part 1: What he would say to Ben Franklin ([00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1dbhz-CcK_s&t=0))

*(The clip starts at the beginning of the answer; the question itself is not included.)*

**Warren Buffett:** I would probably say to Ben Franklin, "You just keep thinking, and don't talk to me, because you'll come up with some better ideas than I will." He was [an] incredibly remarkable person. I've been there. He's almost, probably, the last person to almost have a grasp of every aspect of activity in the country. He invented all kinds of things.

And incidentally, we were talking about the power of compounding and that sort of thing. He left a will that left a sum of money to Philadelphia and another sum to Boston that would serve as an example, for a couple hundred years, of the power of compounding, and all kinds of things. So, I mean, he was so far ahead of his time that the best thing I could do, if I was under that tree with him, was to get out of his way and let him just keep thinking.

## Part 3: Humility, and inventions that become uncontrollable: the atom bomb ([02:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1dbhz-CcK_s&t=166))

**Warren Buffett:** I would say that I would advise Ben to figure out how to win the game and keep a certain amount of humility at the same time. And I would tell him to try to find a system that doesn't invent too many things that can destroy the planet, that become uncontrollable once you get them out there.

There was no alternative to us developing the atom bomb, but the expansion of the number of people that have the ability, from one to eight, and nine probably pretty soon with Iran, I mean, that's a mistake that society just cannot afford to make. I mean, solving the problem with nine variables instead of simply one.

Now, it's totally understandable. My dad was in Congress when the atom bomb was first used. It's amazing how Sam Rayburn kept the House of Representatives uninformed, because they're supposed to appropriate all the money, and [they] had 435 congressmen there, and they had no idea they were appropriating money for Los Alamos, or what was going on in Chicago, or what was going on in Tennessee. But anyway.

## Part 4: America has moved in the right direction, but faces new problems ([04:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1dbhz-CcK_s&t=262))

**Warren Buffett:** We do have a society that is far beyond anything that Ben Franklin dreamt of. It's achieved some of the [...] or it's moving in the right direction toward solving some problems, where we made kind of broad declarations about all men being created equal, etc., and then we did some of the things we did. But generally speaking, we've moved in the right direction.

But we face problems that, I don't know how Ben Franklin would attack the problem of what you do once you get weapons of mass destruction in many hands, and when you essentially look at the world as something where there are winners and losers, and the winners humiliate the losers and do all kinds of things. [...] But all of the people that are a lot younger can figure out the answers on that.

But it's still the most wonderful [...] There's never been anything you could dream [of] like what has happened in the United States.
