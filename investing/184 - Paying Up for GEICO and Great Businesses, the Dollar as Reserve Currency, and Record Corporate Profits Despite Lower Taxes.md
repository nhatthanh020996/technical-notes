# Paying Up for GEICO and Great Businesses, the Dollar as Reserve Currency, and Record Corporate Profits Despite Lower Taxes

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pVb4X5fmwdM

Recorded: Part 1: 2013 Berkshire annual meeting (high confidence); Parts 2–3: probably the same 2013 meeting, possibly another meeting of roughly 2011–2014 (low–moderate confidence)

> Note: The original YouTube title is "Warren Buffett: Why High PE Ratios Are A Strong Buy Signal". It is misleading: nobody says a high P/E is a buy signal. Buffett says only that Berkshire has "paid up" for good businesses (about 20 times earnings for GEICO) more than it would have 30 or 40 years ago, and that paying up for extraordinary businesses "is not a mistake". Munger then makes the short seller's case that giant companies rarely keep doing well, and the clip moves on to the dollar as reserve currency and corporate profits and taxes, which have nothing to do with P/E ratios.
>
> Date: Part 1 is from the **2013** Berkshire annual meeting (high confidence). The clues: Buffett says that "in few months we'll own half of another one" (Heinz, announced in February 2013 and closed in June 2013), and he replies to "Doug" (Doug Kass, the short-seller panelist at the 2013 meeting) and to Munger's "short sellers argument". Parts 2 and 3 are separate questions and may come from the same meeting or a nearby year (low–moderate confidence): the economy "come back very, very strong" in profits from "the fall of 2008" while employment has not, record corporate profits as a share of GDP, and Munger's reference to "Warren's idea that people like us should pay more" (the "Buffett Rule" debate from 2011 on) all fit 2011–2014. The Part 2 questioner says "on our side of the world", so they may be from outside the US.
>
> Notation: `[word]` = added by editor for readability; `[...]` = speaker trails off; `[unclear: "..."]` = garbled in the captions, kept verbatim; `[speaker uncertain]` = attribution inferred.

## Part 3: Record corporate profits, a halved corporate tax, and inequality ([04:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pVb4X5fmwdM&t=286))

*(The clip starts in the middle of Buffett's answer; the question, which apparently cited profit figures as a percentage of GDP, is not included.)*

**Warren Buffett:** Now, the corporate income tax is about half what it was 40 years ago as a percentage of GDP. Yet, as you point out, corporate profits are at an all-time record as a percentage of GDP. So, I would have you take with a grain of salt the complaint that American business is non-competitive because of our corporate income tax rate, which gets so widely complained about. American business has done extraordinarily well at a time when inequality actually has widened considerably, both measured by net worth and measured by income, if you take the top versus the people down below.

And, oh, we heard from one of the people here. And it will be interesting to see whether these levels can be maintained. Corporate businesses come back very, very strong in terms of profits from the precipice that we were on in the fall of 2008, the panic.

Employment has not come back the same way, and that's going to be, I would say, a subject of a lot of public discourse, and you're seeing, you're reading more about that currently. If I had to bet on whether corporate profits would be 10% of GDP, and of course we're talking about profits that are earned outside the United States. I believe in the figures you quote. I would say they're likely to trend downward, but I think that of course GDP will be growing, so that does not mean any terrible things will be happening to profits. Charlie, what do you think about that?

**Charlie Munger:** Well, I wouldn't be too surprised if that 6% figure turns out to be on the low side in the estimate. Just [because] we [unclear: "were only thought something"] 20 years ago doesn't mean it's a law of nature.

**Warren Buffett:** We'll talk this over at lunchtime. How do you feel about 10%?

**Charlie Munger:** Well, I'm a natural conservative on such items. But you got to recognize that the stocks themselves are owned by a lot of endowments and pension funds and so on. That figure doesn't mean that the world's becoming grossly more unequal. There's no automatic correlation between those two figures.

**Warren Buffett:** Do you feel the corporate tax rate is too high?

**Charlie Munger:** Well, I think when the rest of the world keeps bringing the rates down, there's some disadvantage to us if we're much higher. So, I rather like Warren's idea that people like us should pay more, but the corporate tax rate I'm glad to have lower.

**Warren Buffett:** Okay, he's the Republican, I'm the Democrat.
