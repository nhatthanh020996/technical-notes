# Texas Pacific Land Trust's Buybacks, Berkshire's Repurchase Principles, and the Myth of Independent Directors

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eo9CH9gKSg0

Recorded: most likely 2023 Berkshire annual meeting (moderate confidence)

> Note: The original YouTube title is "Warren Buffett: Why Stocks Are The Best Way To Get Rich". It is misleading: nothing in the clip argues that stocks are the best way to get rich. Buffett tells the story of Texas Pacific Land Trust (his second stock), which kept buying in its own shares, then states his principles for Berkshire's own repurchases, and the second half is about why "independent" directors who depend on board fees are not really independent (a letter from a director whose income was 100% board fees, and institutions voting against Buffett at Coca-Cola). The clip is most likely from the **2023** Berkshire annual meeting (moderate confidence); it may be stitched from more than one answer, but the parts appear to come from the same meeting. The clues: Buffett says "I haven't lived to be 100 yet" about a calculation he made at 13 or 14, and both men reflect on "having come a considerable distance from small beginnings", which fits their final meetings together; director pay of "$300,000 a year" and Munger's remark that boards now want "one horse, one rabbit, one cow" (diversity requirements) are recent; and Buffett says "we're going to have a shareholders meeting just a couple minutes", which fits the modern format where the formal business meeting follows the Q&A. The Coca-Cola vote he describes happened in 2004 (he himself says "I forget whether it was 2004 or 2000").
