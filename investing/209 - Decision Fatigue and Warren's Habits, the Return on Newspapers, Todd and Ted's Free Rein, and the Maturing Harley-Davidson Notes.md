# Decision Fatigue and Warren's Habits, the Return on Newspapers, Todd and Ted's Free Rein, and the Maturing Harley-Davidson Notes

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e0MQ34JHhUc

Recorded: Parts 2–4 most likely 2013 Berkshire annual meeting (moderate-high confidence); Part 1 probably the same meeting (low-moderate confidence)

> Note: The original YouTube title is "Charlie Munger: Don't Overthink When Choosing Investments". It is misleading: nobody says that, and nothing here is about how to choose investments. Munger's only substantial remarks (Part 1) are about decision fatigue: he says Warren and he live "on autopilot" in the ordinary decisions of life so they don't waste decision-making energy, and that Warren never makes important decisions when tired. The rest of the clip is Buffett on the expected return from Berkshire's newspaper purchases, the free rein given to "Todd and Ted" (presumably Berkshire's investment managers Todd Combs and Ted Weschler; surnames are not in the captions), and the Harley-Davidson notes.
>
> The clip appears to be stitched from one Berkshire annual meeting. ## Part 2: What return Berkshire expects from its newspapers ([01:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e0MQ34JHhUc&t=115))

**Questioner (read by a journalist):** The following question comes from a shareholder who asked to remain anonymous. Don't you think there are other businesses with higher rates of return that you could buy? Why would you buy such a small business, since you always say you want to buy elephants? **Warren Buffett:** Yeah, I would say that we will get a decent rate of return, whether it's [...] Most of them, incidentally, have been bought [...] and they were either S corporations or partnerships of some sort. I think it's very unlikely that it would be significantly lower. [unclear: "uh it doesn't have it."] It's not going to move the needle at Berkshire, you know. ## Part 4: The Harley-Davidson notes and crisis-era deals as a depleting asset ([07:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e0MQ34JHhUc&t=467))

**Questioner:** Okay. Back in 2009, you made a substantial investment in Harley-Davidson with a 5-year term at 15%. I noticed that note comes due in 2014. What are your plans or thoughts once that investment comes due?

**Warren Buffett:** Well, what we'd like to do is not answer the mail and just let them keep paying us 15%. But that won't happen. No, those were [...] we had a few private transactions during a period when the corporate bond market was basically frozen, and received unusual terms, although the best terms those companies obviously could have obtained at that time. And those deals are coming to [...] and I wish the five-year deals have been 10-year deals. But, you know, that was a special time, and in effect that's a depleting asset that we have that's left over from five years ago.

We won't see anything like that for a while, but we'll see similar things at some point in the future. I mean, the world is given to excesses, and they have consequences, and we are always willing to act. I mean, we did not think Harley-Davidson was going to go broke. I mean, it was that simple. But, you know, any kind of company that gets its customers to tattoo ads on their chest can't be all bad, you know? I mean [...] but it will be a sad day when the Harley-Davidson notes mature. Okay, Becky.
