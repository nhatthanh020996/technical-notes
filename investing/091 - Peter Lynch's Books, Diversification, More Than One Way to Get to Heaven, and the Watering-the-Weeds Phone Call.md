# Peter Lynch's Books, Diversification, More Than One Way to Get to Heaven, and the Watering-the-Weeds Phone Call

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p16OA6Rl-Jo

Recorded: Part 1 most likely a mid-1990s Berkshire annual meeting (low-moderate confidence); Part 2 a Peter Lynch interview, date not shown

> Note: The original YouTube title is "Warren Buffett: Peter Lynch Is An Unparalleled Investor". It is an exaggeration: Buffett never calls Lynch "unparalleled". He says Lynch has "an outstanding record" and is "a high-grade guy", that he doesn't know him well, and that their methods differ (Lynch diversifies much more; "there's more than one way to get to heaven"; neither would do as well using the other's method). The clip is a compilation of two parts. Part 1 is a Berkshire meeting question: the questioner has read "the two books by Peter Lynch" (*One Up on Wall Street*, 1989, and *Beating the Street*, 1993), and Buffett calls the next microphone as "zone two", as was done at 1990s meetings. This suggests a **mid-1990s** meeting (low-moderate confidence). Part 2 is a separate clip in which Peter Lynch (identified from the content: he recounts Buffett calling him about "your book") tells how Buffett phoned him in 1989 to ask to use his "watering the weeds" line (editor's background: the line appears in Buffett's 1989 shareholder letter). The interview's date and venue are not shown. The questioner's name is kept as captioned.

## Part 1: Buffett on Peter Lynch and his books ([00:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p16OA6Rl-Jo&t=1))

**Questioner (Mike Macy, Indianapolis):** I have really enjoyed reading your annual letters and your annual report, and I've gone back and read all the older ones too. They're terrific. I have also enjoyed reading the two books by Peter Lynch, and I see a lot of commonalities between the two of you, the way you think and your philosophy, etc. I'd certainly appreciate it if you'd make a few comments on what you think of Peter Lynch, the things he says in his two books and the advice that he gives to investors.

**Warren Buffett:** I know Peter. I don't know him well, but I [...] we played bridge together in Omaha, as a matter of fact, and I like him personally, and obviously he has an outstanding record, and he has written those two books, which have been bestsellers, about his investment philosophy. I don't really have anything, you know, I'm not going to embroider on his [...]

There's certainly a fair amount of overlap. There's some difference. Peter obviously likes to diversify a lot more than I do. I mean, he owns more stocks than the names of companies I can remember. I mean, but that's Peter. And I, you know, I've said in investing in the past that there's more than one way to get to heaven, and that there isn't a true religion in this, but there's some very useful religions, and Peter's got one, and I think that we've got one that's useful too.

And there is a lot of overlap, but I would not do as well if I tried to do it the way Peter does it, and he probably wouldn't do as well if he tried to do it exactly the way I do it. I like him personally very much. He's a high-grade guy. Zone two.
