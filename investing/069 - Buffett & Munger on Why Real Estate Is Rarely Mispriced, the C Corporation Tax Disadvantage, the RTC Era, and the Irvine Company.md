# Buffett & Munger on Why Real Estate Is Rarely Mispriced, the C Corporation Tax Disadvantage, the RTC Era, and the Irvine Company

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gxK2vXKlFO8

Recorded: a Berkshire annual meeting, probably late 1990s to mid-2000s (low confidence)

> Note: The original YouTube title is "Warren Buffett: Real Estate Is A Very Poor Choice Of Investment". It is misleading. Buffett does not say real estate is a poor investment in general; he says developed real estate tends to be accurately priced, so mispricings are rare except in chaos like the RTC period. The "very lousy investment" line is Munger's, and it is narrower: real estate is lousy *for a taxable C corporation like Berkshire*, because of the extra layer of corporate tax, and Berkshire has no special competence in the field. Both men say they personally made money in real estate (Munger made his early money there). The year cannot be pinned down from the clip. It is a Berkshire annual meeting (Buffett mentions people "in this room", Munger refers to "a corporation such as ours"). The RTC (Resolution Trust Corporation, early 1990s) is discussed as past history, and REIT transactions are described as current, so a guess would be the late 1990s to mid-2000s (low confidence). The clip opens with a short teaser made of two sentences that recur later in the answer.

## Part 3: Munger: no competitive advantage, and the C corporation tax layer ([02:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gxK2vXKlFO8&t=143))

**Charlie Munger:** Yeah, we don't have any competitive advantage over experienced real estate investors in the field. And we wouldn't have if we were operating with our own money as a partnership.

And if you operate as a corporation such as ours, which is taxable under chapter C of the Internal Revenue Code, you get a whole layer of corporate taxes between the real estate income and the use of the income by the people who own the real estate. So, by its nature, real estate tends to be a very lousy investment for people who are taxed under subchapter C of the code relating to corporations.

So, the combination of having it [be a] generally lousy activity for people with our tax structure and having no special competence in the field means that we spend almost no time thinking about anything in real estate.
