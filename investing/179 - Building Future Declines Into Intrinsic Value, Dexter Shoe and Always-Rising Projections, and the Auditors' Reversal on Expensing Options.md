# Building Future Declines Into Intrinsic Value, Dexter Shoe and Always-Rising Projections, and the Auditors' Reversal on Expensing Options

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VtbJo0zzEis

Recorded: 2003 Berkshire annual meeting (high confidence)

> Note: The original YouTube title is "Warren Buffett Explains How To Calculate Intrinsic Value (For Beginners)". It is misleading: there is no calculation method and nothing aimed at beginners. Buffett says intrinsic value is "terribly important" and "very fuzzy", and uses two gas pipelines to make one point: if competition will force price cuts later, a proper calculation already builds that decline in today. He then mocks investment bankers' projections that always go up, admits the Dexter Shoe mistake, and Munger and Buffett discuss why they would refuse to manage money against indexes and consultants. The last third (from 05:15) is a separate exchange about the failure to expense stock options and the big auditing firms switching sides, which has nothing to do with calculating intrinsic value. The clip is from the **2003** Berkshire annual meeting (high confidence). The clues: Buffett contrasts the auditors' 1993 lobbying with their position "in 2003"; he refers to only four auditing firms being left (after Arthur Andersen's collapse in 2002); and Dexter Shoe is discussed as a known mistake. The upload date (July 2025) is not the event date. The clip looks edited from at least two separate answers (the jump at 05:15).

## Part 2: Investment bankers' projections that never go down, and the Dexter Shoe mistake ([02:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VtbJo0zzEis&t=120))

**Warren Buffett:** that's part of predicting in business. I mean, I really have never seen an investment banker's book [...] I hope to see one someday, and I hope I can survive the shock when I do see it, where the earnings of the business being offered go down. Lots of businesses' earnings go down, and they're going to go down. And I get all this nonsense, you know, where they project it out for 10 years and it always goes up. It just isn't the real world.

And you have to analyze businesses. Some businesses are going to be subjected to enormous competitive pressures that aren't [unclear: "extent"; context suggests "extant"] today. And we made that mistake, for example, at Dexter Shoe. I mean, we bought a business that was earning $40 million or so pre-tax, and we assumed that the future would be as good as the past, and I couldn't have been more wrong. So that was a case of projecting into the future conditions which were not going to exist in the future, competitive conditions. That's part of business.

And I will tell you that 20% of the Fortune 500, but I don't know which 20%, are going to be earning significantly less money, probably, 5 years from now than they are today. And that's what the game is all about: figuring out what those future cash flows are likely to be. And when you feel you can't come up with reasonable estimates in that respect, you move on to the next one. Charlie?

## Part 6: The four auditing firms switch sides, and the Vicar of Bray ([06:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VtbJo0zzEis&t=381))

**Warren Buffett:** It's really astounding. The interesting thing is, of course, now is that the four auditing firms left, what would they call [them], the final four now in the auditing [...] they have now, and listen, I'm glad they did it too, and I tip my hat to them for doing it, but they've now said that they really do think options are [an] expense.

So it kind of reminds you of what happened during the Reformation, isn't it? When you'd have these places sway back and forward as they get carried by one argument or the other. And in fact, I think there was that famous Vicar of Bray, who would swing from Catholicism to Luther, back and forth, as this little town went back and forth, in Germany. And finally the townspeople gathered, and they said to the vicar, they said, "It's understandable that we're confused by all that's going on in theology, and we really don't know much about it, and so we swing back and forth. But," they said, "we find it a little disgusting that you, a man of the cloth, would also keep swaying back and forth." And they said, "Have you no principle?" And he said, "Yes, I have one principle." He says, "It's to remain the Vicar of Bray."

Well, I think we've seen a little of that in the auditing profession, but I think they've actually found the true religion now. So I don't want to sit here and criticize them. But you now have four firms that lobbied against options being counted as an expense in 1993 that have written the FASB and say that options should be an expense. And I don't know how in the world something could have not been an expense in 1993 and be an expense in 2003. Certainly didn't apply to utility bills or raw materials or anything of the sort. But that's the human condition. Number two.
