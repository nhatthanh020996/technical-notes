# Judging Banks by Their Culture, Why Banks Were Not the Problem in 2020, and Lower Bank Returns After the Crisis

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9LCTXtq7jgo

Recorded: compilation of Berkshire annual meetings. Part 2: 2020 (high confidence); Parts 3-5: 2011 (moderate-high confidence); Part 1: roughly 2009-2011 (low-moderate confidence)

> Note: The original YouTube title is "Warren Buffett: The Panic Around Big Bank Stocks Is Totally Irrational". It is misleading: nobody in the clip talks about a panic in big bank stocks or calls anything irrational. Part 1 is about small regional banks, and it is Munger who says the big banks' disgrace has "pounded down the stocks of some small banks" where nothing is wrong. In Part 2 Buffett says the banks "are not a primary worry of mine at all", but adds that he "wouldn't say that with a hundred percent certainty"; in Parts 3-4 he says bank returns on equity "will be less" than before the crisis. The clip is a compilation of at least three Berkshire meetings, which the uploader does not say:
>
> - **Part 1** is probably from around **2009-2011** (low-moderate confidence). The clues: Munger says very large banks "both here [and] in Europe" have "cast a pall of disgrace over the whole industry", and he mentions the "Florida real estate situation"; Buffett names Berkshire's bank stocks as Wells Fargo, U.S. Bank and M&T.
> - **Part 2** is from the **2020** Berkshire annual meeting (high confidence): Buffett compares Jerome Powell's job "this time" with 2008-9, says "we decided as a people to shut down part of the economy", mentions banks building reserves "in the first quarter" and problems with energy loans, and refers to Jamie Dimon's JPMorgan letter. Munger did not attend that meeting.
> - **Parts 3-5** are from the **2011** Berkshire annual meeting (moderate-high confidence). The clues: Buffett points to an HBO showing in late May (the film "Too Big to Fail" premiered on HBO in May 2011; the captions say "May 26th"), the FDIC has closed "250 of them or so in the last couple years", loan losses "have been trending downward now for several quarters", and both Buffett and Munger recommend Bob Wilmers's M&T letter, whose criticism of big banks' trading profits matches his 2010 letter (published in 2011).
