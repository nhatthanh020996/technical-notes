# Buffett & Munger on Consultants, Fees, and Why Index Fund Investors Beat the Professionals

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XGIkQ9bsWZA

> Note: The original YouTube title is "Warren Buffett Reveals The Ugly Truth About Hedge Funds". It is misleading: there is no secret "ugly truth" revealed and hedge funds are only touched on indirectly (the "2 and 20" fee). The clip is about consultants and money managers whose fees eat up the returns that a passive S&P 500 index fund investor gets automatically, plus Munger's comment that good managers are a needle in a haystack. The clip is most likely from the **2017** Berkshire annual meeting (moderate–high confidence). The clues: Buffett refers to a 10-year period on which he "stuck [his] neck out" (his 10-year bet against hedge funds, which ended in 2017), contrasts "this group over here" with "that part of the Auditorium" (a hypothetical split of the audience he used at the 2017 meeting), mentions the book *Where Are the Customers' Yachts?* being sold at the meeting bookstore, and closes with "we'll come back at one o'clock" (the lunch break at a Berkshire meeting).

## Part 1: Why rich investors and their consultants don't just buy the index ([00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XGIkQ9bsWZA&t=0))

*(The clip starts mid-answer; the question and the start of Buffett's answer are not included.)*

**Warren Buffett:** But it seems so elementary, but I will guarantee you that no endowment fund, no public pension fund, no extremely rich person wants to sit in that part of the Auditorium. They just can't believe that because they have billions of dollars to invest, they can't go out and hire somebody who will do better than average. I hear from them all the time.

So this group over here, supposedly sophisticated people, generally richer people, hire consultants, and no consultant in the world is going to tell you "just buy an S&P index fund and sit for the next 50 years." You don't get to be a consultant that way, and you certainly don't get an annual fee that way. So the consultant's got every motivation in the world to tell you, "This year I think we should concentrate more on international stocks," "This year this manager is particularly good on the short side." And so they come in and they talk for hours, and you pay them a large fee, and they always suggest something other than just sitting on your rear end and participating in American business without costs. And then those consultants, after they get their fees, they in turn recommend to you other people who charge fees, which, as you can see, over a period of time cumulatively eat up capital like crazy.

So I would suggest that what I felt sure [...] I didn't feel sure, because nothing [unclear: "can't tell for sure"] about any 10-year period, but I've certainly felt [it] very probable, or I wouldn't have stuck my neck out. It just demonstrates so dramatically.

## Part 4: Munger: good managers are a needle in a haystack ([03:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XGIkQ9bsWZA&t=191))

**Charlie Munger:** We are talking to a bunch of people who have solved their problem by buying Berkshire Hathaway. That worked even better. And there have been a few of these managers who've actually succeeded. There are a few in the universities who are really good. But it's a tiny group of people. It's like looking for a needle in a haystack.

**Warren Buffett:** Yeah. And when I was given the job of naming two in 1969, I knew two. I knew a couple of others. Charlie wasn't interested in managing more money. My friend Walter Schloss would not [...] he would not scale up well, although he had a fabulous record over 45 years or thereabouts. But that was all I could come up with at that time. And fortunately, I did have a couple, and the people who went with Sequoia Fund have been well served if they stayed for the whole period.

But there's been far, far more money made by people in Wall Street through salesmanship abilities than through investment abilities. There are a few people out there that are going to have an outstanding investment record, but there are very few of them, and the people you pay to have identified them don't know how to identify them. And they do know how to sell you. And that's my message.

We'll come back at one o'clock. Thanks.
