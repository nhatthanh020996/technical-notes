# Students Chasing Hedge Funds and Private Equity, the Pause in Phone Calls After Succession, and Why Berkshire's Culture Will Outlast Buffett

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DeYQLcYLXDA

Recorded: most likely 2007 Berkshire annual meeting, possibly 2006–2010 (low-moderate confidence)

> Note: The original YouTube title is "Warren Buffett: Economics Departments Teach Useless Nonsense". It is misleading: Buffett never says that. He says that 25 years earlier you had to go along with the efficient-markets and modern-portfolio-theory orthodoxy to get ahead in a *finance* department, and that this "has improved a lot"; the rest of the clip is about students all wanting to go into private equity and hedge funds, and then a long answer on succession: after Buffett, sellers' phone calls may slow for a year or two, he opposes splitting the chairman/CEO roles while he is alive, and he expects the culture to stay the same (comparing it to Walmart after Sam Walton). The clip is most likely from a Berkshire annual meeting around **2007** (low–moderate confidence). The clues: Charlie is alive and on stage; Buffett says "there's three candidates" for CEO, which matches what he said publicly from about 2006 to 2010 about three internal candidates; Munger's remark that about half of elite business-school graduates want to go into private equity or hedge funds fits the pre-2008 boom; Buffett offers students "two B shares". One questioner calls Buffett a "70 years young teenager" in the captions, which would point to about 2000–2001, but the rest of the content fits later better, so this may be a caption error or loose phrasing. The upload date (October 2025) is not the event date.

## Part 1: How finance teaching has changed, and the students who visit Omaha ([00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DeYQLcYLXDA&t=0))

*(The clip starts mid-answer; the question is not included.)*

**Warren Buffett:** 25 years ago you'd have had a tough job getting a position in a finance department, and you certainly would have had your advancement stifled unless you went along with the orthodoxy: efficient markets and modern portfolio theory and a lot of stuff that not only wouldn't do you any good but might get you in trouble. And that's improved a lot. And I enjoy seeing these groups of students as they come out, because it's quite encouraging. Now, they all think they're going to get rich by sort of copying what Charlie and I did many, many years ago.

I wish them well. The amount of brain power going into money management gets a little distressing, particularly to Charlie. But it's a great time to be 22 or 23 or 25 and getting out of school. So you can look ahead to a very, I think, a very interesting future in this country, even though you may find that the method of using the talents you have in investing get used in a somewhat different manner than where they're used presently. I mean, right now an awful lot of the students that come to visit Omaha say private equity or hedge fund, than [...] It's hard to imagine a world where everybody's running a hedge fund. I'm not sure exactly what we would do for food and clothing and a few things like that.

But I am encouraged by the kind of students I meet. And when the KU group comes up, we had a great time. They put on various skits. They try to sell me companies. I'm hoping they succeed. We haven't had any luck with [it] yet, but they keep coming up with good ideas and I'll keep pursuing them. And one of these days every one of those students will get [...] I've offered them two B shares, and it's a limited time offer, to try and spur extra activity.

And I hear from a lot of students later, and I think a lot of them have their heads screwed on right. Charlie?

**Questioner:** Swamiji, I have a question. ## Part 4: The successors, and why a half-and-half chairman/CEO split wouldn't work ([05:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DeYQLcYLXDA&t=357))

**Warren Buffett:** I can tell you the successors that the board has in mind, you know, they're very smart. They understand. They bought in to the whole corporate personality we have, and they will be somewhat different in style, but they will develop the confidence of the world that it's going to be the same Berkshire going forward. But it's a good question, and there will be a period when the phone won't ring for a while, till people realize that Berkshire is sort of one of a kind and it's continuing to be one of a kind.

I don't think it would work well. It's [the] kind of thing we talk about with the board of [...] but I don't think it works well to have sort of a half-and-half arrangement. I mean, you could say that I could handle or encourage the handling of the deals and somebody else could sort of be the operating guy. But the truth is, we don't need an operating guy. We've got people running the businesses that are running them, and they're very good at it, and the main thing to do is to not destroy or damage the spirit they bring to it, and the fact that they like this method of operation. So I'm not sure what a chief operating officer would do at Berkshire, except expose the fact that I wasn't doing anything.

And as long as I'm around, they're not going to get the calls on the [...] I mean, people are going to want to talk to me. That's not a handoff that would work. So I think we'll go along in this mode.

And you will have a period, everybody. There will be stories a year after I die that, you know, says "1 year later" and what's happened and all that sort of thing. But that will fade out. And my successor will put his own particular stamp on the place. But he won't mess with the culture. And they're too smart. They've seen it work too well. So the calls will start coming in again after a while.

## Part 5: Still one of a kind for owners, the Munger heirs, and the Sam Walton comparison ([08:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DeYQLcYLXDA&t=503))

**Warren Buffett:** We will still represent sort of a one-of-a-kind place for the owner that really cares about the future of his business. For one reason or another, tax reasons, family, division of shares or something, they have to solve the ownership problem, but they want to solve it in a way that really doesn't change the psychic ownership of the place and the management of the place. And they can't find that elsewhere, and they'll continue to find it at Berkshire. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger:** Well, speaking for the Munger heirs, I would rather the current method of operation continue, to wring the last drop of good out of Warren.

**Warren Buffett:** *[speaker uncertain]* At low pay.

**Charlie Munger:** *[speaker uncertain]* Yes. *[laughter]* Yes.

**Warren Buffett:** Part of Charlie's instructions under all circumstances.

Oh, if we thought there was some better way to make this place function better, or to even make the transition easier to the next person, we'd be delighted. But I think we think it's going to work pretty darn well. If I died tonight, the person who will take over tomorrow will not get as many phone calls for a while, perhaps. But very, very smart people, know the business. And they know a lot about all businesses. They got a general business knowledge. I just use "they" 'cause there's three candidates, but there would be one specific one of mine. They know how to make deals. I mean, these people are plenty deal savvy. And they know how to avoid other kinds of deals, which is equally important.

And the world would not fully grasp that for a year, maybe even 2 years. But once it happened, you could argue that it would be even stronger than before, because at that point people would realize it was institutionalized and not just a person.

I mean, you had a kind of a [unclear: "hat"]. I mean, I don't want to compare myself, 'cause it's not in the same league, but everybody, when Sam Walton died, and I think what, 1991 or something, they wondered whether Walmart would continue in the same tradition. Well, the fact that it did [unclear: "is so made"] that place a lot stronger than if it had just depended on the guy in the pickup truck. I mean, it was the creation of one person, of Walmart, but it was not required for the continuation at all. And we're not in the same league, but it's the same idea.
