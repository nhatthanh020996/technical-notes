## Part 5: Printing money, war bonds, and the political temptation of deficits ([10:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rGNUoYijz6Q&t=602))

**Charlie Munger:** Well, I don't know where we're headed with all of this. It's been very extreme. I think that you could be pretty extreme in fighting depressions and so forth if you reverted afterwards to a period of some discipline. But if you're going to just keep borrowing, printing money and spending it, I think eventually it causes bad trouble. And you can see it in Latin America. Latin America let its currency get out of control all the time, and of course it lagged the United States in economic achievement greatly. So I think we pay a price if we ever give up our old ways entirely and go into a new world where we just try and print money to make it easier to get through the year.

We paid a price in World War II. I mean, everybody, school kids and everybody else, myself included, we bought what [were] originally called war bonds and defense bonds and savings bonds and all that. From [unclear: "19402"; likely 1942] to 1944 or '5, you paid out [unclear: "1875"; likely $18.75] and you got $25 back, and every kid [bought] savings [unclear: "STS"; likely "stamps"] and all that. But when you got all through, you had 120% of GDP in the national debt instead of 30 or 40%, and we had a lot of inflation subsequently. A lot. So the people that bought those bonds and supported the war had a portion of their purchasing power taken away from them. Well, there wasn't anything wrong with that particularly, but when the country gets in the habit of doing that [...] I think it's tough to figure out where the breaking point is with society, but I don't think you want to come anywhere close to it.

**Warren Buffett:** Yeah. It's also tough to have a mass of people unemployed. That's the tension.

**Charlie Munger:** *[speaker uncertain]* Yeah, well, that's why the Fed has two [objectives], in terms of employment and inflation.

**Warren Buffett:** *[speaker uncertain]* But they are not the ones that create the deficit. And so far the system has worked pretty well, although, like I say, it's been so far [like] the man who jumped off a tall building.

**Charlie Munger:** *[speaker uncertain]* Yeah, [he] is all right until he hits the ground.

**Warren Buffett:** Yeah, well, but there could be ways we can stop now at the third floor or the sixth floor. We don't know what floor it is, but we know it isn't happening at the ground.
