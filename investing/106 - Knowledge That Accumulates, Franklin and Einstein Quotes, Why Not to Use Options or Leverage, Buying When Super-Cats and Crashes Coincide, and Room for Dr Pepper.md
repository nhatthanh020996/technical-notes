# Knowledge That Accumulates, Franklin and Einstein Quotes, Why Not to Use Options or Leverage, Buying When Super-Cats and Crashes Coincide, and Room for Dr Pepper

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8wA0L6-2cUw

Recorded: a mid-to-late-1990s Berkshire annual meeting, possibly around 1997 (low confidence)

> Note: The original YouTube title is "Warren Buffett: How To Understand The Market". It is misleading: nobody explains how to understand "the market." Buffett says they understand *businesses* through knowledge accumulated over decades, and that focusing on short-term price behavior takes your eye off "the main ball which is just valuing businesses." The rest of the clip covers general education and favorite quotations, why investors should avoid options, index futures and leverage, hazardous-waste stocks, what Berkshire would do if super-cat losses and a market crash came in the same year, and whether soft drinks are a winner-take-all business. The clip is from a Berkshire annual meeting (high confidence: shareholder questioners, numbered "zones"/"areas" for microphones, and Buffett closing with "it's 3:30 ... I look forward to seeing you next year"). The year is uncertain. The clues point to the mid-to-late 1990s: Buffett says "if you started doing it 40 or so years ago"; questions about OEX index options and LEAPS (introduced around 1990); Berkshire writing "super cats" (a big business for Berkshire in the 1990s); Sprite doing "well over a billion cases a year"; and Munger mentioning a letter Buffett wrote opposing the new options/futures exchanges (Buffett's 1982 letter to Congress). My guess is around 1996–1998. The segments may come from one meeting or be stitched from several; the end of the meeting is included, which suggests at least the last part is from one session.
