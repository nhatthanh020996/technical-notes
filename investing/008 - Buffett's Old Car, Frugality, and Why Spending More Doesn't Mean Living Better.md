# Buffett's Old Car, Frugality, and Why Spending More Doesn't Mean Living Better

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bFWbvlRPBvc

Recorded: compilation. Car answer: possibly 1990s (low confidence); frugality exchange: Berkshire annual meeting, undated

> Note: The original YouTube title is "Warren Buffett: Why I'm Never Buying A New Car". It is misleading. Buffett says he would happily drive a newer car if it were safer or if switching cost him no time. He just doesn't want to spend half a day buying one for no benefit. The clip seems to be stitched together from more than one segment: a short answer about Buffett's car, then an exchange with Charlie Munger about frugality that ends at a Berkshire annual meeting. The meeting year can't be determined from the transcript. The comment that his car may be the first of its type with airbags on both sides suggests the 1990s, but that is weak evidence, and the frugality part may come from a different year.

## Part 1: Why Buffett keeps his old car

*(The clip starts mid-answer. The question was presumably about Buffett's car and its safety.)*

**Warren Buffett:** I actually picked out the car I have based on the fact that it had airbags on both sides, so that was a factor. It may be the first car of its type ever made with airbags. I think my car is both heavy and has airbags, and those are two primary factors in safety. I don't think a safer car is necessarily being made. It might be safer to drive around in a big heavy-duty truck or something, but I'm not ready for that.

It's an ordinary old car. I look at that like anything else. It would take me probably half a day to go through the exercise of buying a car and reading the owner's manual and all of that. And that's just half a day I don't want to give up in my life for no benefit. If I could write a check in 30 seconds and be in the same position I'm in now with a newer car, I'd be glad to do it this afternoon. But I don't like to trade away time when there's really no benefit to me at all. I'm totally happy with the car. I just don't want to trade away the amount of time I'd have to spend fooling around to pick out a new car, get familiar with it, get title to it, and do all the rest of the things. But if there's a safer car made, I'll be driving in it.

## Part 2: Frugality, the houses, and standard of living

**Charlie Munger:** Well, in personal consumption, Warren is more frugal.

**Warren Buffett:** Would you care to give an example?

**Charlie Munger:** Warren lives in the same house he bought for a very modest price in, what, 1950-something?

**Warren Buffett:** I bought it in 1958. And you moved into yours about 1960, didn't you?

**Charlie Munger:** Yeah, and I paid more.

**Warren Buffett:** But he designed his own house. He did not pay an architect, right?

**Charlie Munger:** I did. I paid an architect $1,900. It was about 30% of the normal price.

**Warren Buffett:** Notice how he remembers the details.

No, I have everything in life I want. It's a very simple thing. There are things money can't buy, but if there's anything money could buy that I wanted, I'd do it this afternoon. I wouldn't have any problem with that at all.

I do not think that standard of living equates with cost of living beyond a certain point. Up to a certain point, there's no question that it does: in terms of having good housing, good health, good health services, good food, transportation, everything. But there's a point where, if anything, I think you start getting an inverse correlation. My life would not be happier, and I'd be worse off, if I had six or eight houses or a whole bunch of different things I could have. It just doesn't correlate. I have everything I want; you can't have more than that. It makes a difference up to a point. You can start thinking a lot differently when you've got X. But when you get to 10X or 100X or 1,000X, it just doesn't make any possible difference. Charlie?

**Charlie Munger:** Frugality basically has helped Berkshire. And I look out at this audience and I see a bunch of understated, frugal people too. We collect you people.

**Warren Buffett:** But forget about it this weekend. The more you buy, the more you save at these prices, folks.
