# The Fair Rate of Capital Gains Tax, the Ovarian Lottery, Talent and Incentives, Munger on John Rawls, and the Future of American Industry

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bvqZjy3dGIk

Recorded: most likely 1997 Berkshire annual meeting (moderate confidence)

> Note: The original YouTube title is "Warren Buffett: Thoughts On Capital Gains Tax". It is only mildly misleading: capital gains tax is the starting point, but Munger answers that part first (some taxation of capital gains is needed for fairness; the fair rate might be "a little lower than it is now but not much lower"), and most of Buffett's time goes to the "ovarian lottery" thought experiment, talent and incentives, and whether American industry can compete. Munger also attacks John Rawls's influence. The clip is most likely from the **1997** Berkshire annual meeting (moderate confidence). The clues: Buffett treats a 28% capital gains rate as the current one (the top rate was 28% until the May 1997 tax cut to 20%); Roberto Goizueta is still running Coca-Cola (he died in October 1997); Bill Gates and Andy Grove are running Microsoft and Intel; Nebraska unemployment is under 3%; and the questioner, Patrick, a New Hampshire torch-maker CEO and former heavyweight boxer, has "just got his PhD fairly recently from Stanford". Guess (not stated in the captions): the questioner is Patrick Byrne, then CEO of Centricut. The "seven billion" figure in the captions does not fit 1990s world population and may be a caption error.
