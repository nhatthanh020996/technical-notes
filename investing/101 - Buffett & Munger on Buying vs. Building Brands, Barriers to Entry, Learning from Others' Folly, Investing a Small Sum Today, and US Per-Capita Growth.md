# Buffett & Munger on Buying vs. Building Brands, Barriers to Entry, Learning from Others' Folly, Investing a Small Sum Today, and US Per-Capita Growth

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V0FuR4mloeM

Recorded: compilation of Berkshire annual meeting clips; Parts 7–8 most likely 2012 (moderate-high confidence); Parts 1–6 after 2008, probably early 2010s (low confidence)

> Note: The original YouTube title is "Charlie Munger: Find Stocks To Own Forever". It is misleading: nobody talks about finding stocks to own forever, and Buffett does most of the talking. The clip covers how hard it is to create a brand (Virgin Cola, Coca-Cola), barriers to entry (BNSF, Buffett's gas station), learning from other people's mistakes, what they could do with $1 million today, and long-run US economic growth. It is a **compilation** of Berkshire annual meeting answers ("station 10" at the end). Dating each part: **Parts 7–8** (economic growth) are most likely the **2012** meeting (moderate–high confidence): Buffett says "when I was 81" (he was 81 until August 2012), GDP per capita is about $48,000, the "slump that we entered into a few years ago" and the "fall of 2008" crash are recent, and business profits as a percent of GDP were at a high "last year". **Parts 1–4** come after Berkshire owned a railroad (BNSF, 2010) and around Lipitor's patent expiry (Pfizer, late 2011), and Part 4 mentions 2008 as past, so most likely early 2010s (low confidence). **Parts 5–6**: after 2008, exact year unknown (low confidence). "Don K" in Part 1 is probably Don Keough, former Coca-Cola president and Berkshire director (a guess).

## Part 7: The power of 2.5% real growth, and a sixfold rise in one lifetime ([08:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V0FuR4mloeM&t=522))

*(The clip appears to cut here to a different answer; the question is not included.)*

**Charlie Munger:** *[speaker uncertain]* That's nowhere near 4% per annum.
