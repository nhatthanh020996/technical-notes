# Advisers Adding No Value, Capricious Rewards Under Capitalism, Closet Indexing, and a Better Second Half of Life

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tpvJzzLAkDs

Recorded: a Berkshire annual meeting, probably in the 2010s (low confidence)

> Note: The original YouTube title is "Warren Buffett: Most Financial Advisors Know Nothing About Investing". It is misleading: nobody says advisers "know nothing about investing". Buffett's point is that investing is a simple game and that people in the business have no incentive to admit they add little value (he would bet on dart-throwing monkeys once fees are taken out); Munger's point is that much of the wealth advisory business charges for skill while delivering "closet indexing". Most of the clip is actually about something else: how capriciously capitalism hands out rewards (Gerald Corrigan's quip about a 26-year-old Salomon trader who "can't even throw a football", Ted Williams' salary versus today's ballplayers), not despairing about human self-interest, and becoming a better person in the second half of life. The clip starts mid-answer; the question is not included. The year is uncertain: it is clearly a Berkshire annual meeting with both Buffett and Munger (the audience "here in the auditorium" saw a Salomon segment in the meeting movie, and Buffett ends by telling shareholders they can quiz Charlie later), and the talk of having "had the luxury of living a long time" and of the "ovarian lottery" suggests the later Buffett–Munger meetings, probably the 2010s (low confidence). The captions are heavily garbled in places, and several speaker changes are inferred.
