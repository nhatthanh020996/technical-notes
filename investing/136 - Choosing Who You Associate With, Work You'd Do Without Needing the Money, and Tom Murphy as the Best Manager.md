# Choosing Who You Associate With, Work You'd Do Without Needing the Money, and Tom Murphy as the Best Manager

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FJw8Q-OuR9o

Recorded: most likely 2025 Berkshire annual meeting (moderate confidence)

> Note: The original YouTube title is "Warren Buffett: How To Rapidly Compound Your Money". It is misleading: Buffett says nothing about compounding money. The only "compounding" he mentions is "a compounding of good intentions and good behavior" when you help people who help you. The answer is about choosing the people you associate with, finding work you would do even if you didn't need the money, not feeling guilty about good luck, and Tom Murphy as the best manager he ever knew. The clip is most likely from the **2025** Berkshire annual meeting (moderate confidence). The clues: Buffett refers to Greg Abel and Ajit Jain by first name as current colleagues; Buffett names Tom Murphy, Sandy Gottesman and Walter Scott as fellows who "have died in the last couple years" (they died in 2021–2022); he says Gottesman worked with him "from 1963 until he died a couple years ago"; he cites 8 billion people in the world; and he refers to telling the GEICO door-knocking story earlier in the meeting. Charlie Munger (died November 2023) is not mentioned, which makes 2024 less likely. The upload date (April 2026) rules out the 2026 meeting.

## Part 1: You go in the direction of the people you associate with ([00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FJw8Q-OuR9o&t=0))

**Questioner:** As a young person interested in investing like myself, I would love to hear your insights, Mr. Buffett. What were some pivotal lessons you learned early in your career, and what advice do you have for young investors who are looking to develop their investment philosophy? Thank you.

**Warren Buffett:** Well, those are good questions. I wish I thought of them myself earlier in my life. Who you associate with is just enormously important, and don't expect that you'll make every decision right on that. I mean, you're going to have your life progress in the general direction of the people that you work with, that you admire, that become your friends.

I mentioned a few fellows that have died in the last couple years. Well, all of those people were people that, if we were working together on something 1/10,000th the size of Berkshire, I mean, they'd be the kind of people you'd choose. They're people that make you want to be better than you are, and you want to hang out with people that are better than you are and that you feel are better than you are, because you're going to go in the direction of the people that you associate with.

And that's something you learn, and of course you learn it late in life. It's hard to really appreciate how important some of those factors are until you get much older. But when you've got people around you like Tom Murphy and [unclear: "my like all like what is his name of"] Sandy Gottesman, Walter Scott, you're just going to live a better life than you do if you just go out and look at somebody just making a lot of money and decide you're going to try and copy him or something of the sort.

## Part 2: A job you'd hold if you didn't need the money, and not feeling guilty about luck ([02:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FJw8Q-OuR9o&t=140))

**Warren Buffett:** So, I would try to be associated with smart people too, where I could learn a lot from them. And I would try to look for something that I would do if I didn't need the money. I mean, what you're really looking for [in] life is something where you've got a job that you'd hold if you didn't need the money, and I've had that, and surely I have for a very, very long time. In fact, all the fellows I named had it. And every one of those ones I named, they always did more than their share, and they never sought more than their share of the credit. They just behaved just the way you'd like anybody you work with. And when you find them, you treasure them.

And when you don't find them, you still keep doing whatever causes you to eat or enables you to eat, but you don't give up on looking around, and you'll find people do wonderful things for you. I mentioned earlier the going down to GEICO and knocking on the door when the door was locked. I mean, who knows what was behind that door that I went in, but in 10 minutes I found that I had a man that was going to be just wonderfully helpful to me. And of course, if somebody's going to be helpful to you, you want to try to figure out ways to be helpful to them. So you get a compounding of good intentions and good behavior. And unfortunately, you can get the reverse of that in life too.

And [...] I was lucky in having a good environment for living that kind of a life, and other people have a whole different environmental situation that [they] have to overcome. But don't feel guilty about your good luck. If you've got [...] Well, if you're living in the United States, you've [...] There are 8 billion people in the world and there's 330 million in the United States.
