# Capitalism Without Unfettered Markets, Smartness vs. Wisdom, the Delta Duck Club, and Keeping Politics Out of Berkshire

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dsy32jt4vI0

Recorded: compilation. Parts 1-4 (up to the Ben Graham answer): 2020 Berkshire annual meeting (high confidence); Parts 6-7: 2019 Berkshire annual meeting (high confidence); the personal-investments question (end of Part 4 and Part 5): probably 2019 Berkshire annual meeting (low-moderate confidence)

> Note: The original YouTube title is "Warren Buffett: Why Capitalism Always Wins". It is misleading: Buffett never says capitalism "always wins". He says the market system "works wonders" but is "brutal if left entirely to itself", that he does not want "unfettered capitalism", that it tends toward winner-takes-all outcomes with "a lot of randomness", and that everybody should share in the prosperity; later he calls himself "a card-carrying capitalist" who believes capitalism involves regulation and taking care of people left behind. Much of the clip is about other things: Ben Graham and smartness versus wisdom, the Delta Duck Club share Buffett once bought, Munger's oil royalty and the oil shares he turned down, and how Berkshire keeps Buffett's politics out of the company. The clip is a **compilation**. Parts 1-4 up to the Ben Graham answer are from the **2020** meeting (high confidence): Greg Abel answers instead of Munger, he mentions the pandemic, and Becky Quick reads a sent-in question. Parts 6-7 are from the **2019** meeting (high confidence): Berkshire "in 54 years" has never given to a presidential candidate, the Democratic presidential candidates' talk of socialism, the venture with JPMorgan and Amazon, and the "2020" election ahead. The personal-investments question is asked to "both of you" and Munger answers, so it cannot be from 2020; it is probably also from 2019 (low-moderate confidence). The splice between the two meetings comes at the end of Part 4, where a garbled phrase leads into the new question. Guess (not in the captions): the "B oil" shares Munger bought are probably Belridge Oil.

## Part 6: Politics, Berkshire's money and lobbying ([08:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dsy32jt4vI0&t=537))

**Warren Buffett:** Andrew?

**Andrew Ross Sorkin:** Warren and Charlie, this is a question [...] actually, we got a handful of questions on this topic. This is probably the best formulation of it. "Warren, you have been a longtime outspoken Democrat. With all the talk about socialism versus capitalism taking place among Democratic presidential candidates, do you anticipate an impact on Berkshire in the form of more regulations, higher corporate taxes, or even calls for breakups among the many companies we own, if they were to win? And how do you think about your own politics as a fiduciary of our company, and at the same time as someone who has said that simply being a business leader doesn't mean you've put your citizenship in a blind trust?"

**Warren Buffett:** I have said that you do not put your citizenship in a blind trust, but you also don't speak on behalf of your company. You do speak as a citizen if you speak, and therefore you have to be careful about when you do speak, because it's going to be assumed you're speaking on behalf of your company. Berkshire Hathaway, certainly in 54 years, has never and will never made a contribution to a presidential candidate. I don't think we've made a contribution to any political candidate, but I don't want to say for 54 years that we don't do it.

Now, we operate in several regulated industries, and our railroad and our utility, as a practical matter, they have to have a presence in Washington and in the state legislature in which they operate. So we have some, a few, I don't know how many, political action committees, which existed when we bought [...] we bought the companies, at subsidiaries. And I think unquestionably they make some contributions, simply to achieve the same access of their competitors. If the trucking industry is going to lobby, I'm sure the railroad industry is going to lobby.

But the general [...] well, the rule is, I mean, that people do not pursue their own political interests with your money here. We've had one or two managers over the years, for example, that would do some fundraising where they were fundraising from people who were suppliers to them or something of the sort, and if I ever find out about it, that ends promptly. But my position at Berkshire is not to be used to further my own political beliefs, but my own political beliefs can be expressed as a person, not as a representative of Berkshire.

When a campaign is important, I don't [...] I try to minimize it, but there [...] it's no secret that in the last election, for example, I raised money. I won't give money [to] PACs.
