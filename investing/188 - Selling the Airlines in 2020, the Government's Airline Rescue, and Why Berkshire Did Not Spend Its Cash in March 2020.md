# Selling the Airlines in 2020, the Government's Airline Rescue, and Why Berkshire Did Not Spend Its Cash in March 2020

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LXJAi7pdHOQ

Recorded: 2021 Berkshire annual meeting (high confidence)

> Note: The original YouTube title is "Charlie Munger: You Will Always Lose Money When Timing The Market". It is misleading. Nobody says you will always lose money by timing the market, and almost all of the clip is Buffett, not Munger. The only remark close to the title is in the first 30 seconds (most likely Munger): that it is "crazy" to expect anyone to hoard money and then spend it all "on the bottom tick" in a crisis, and that it is "too tough a standard". The rest is Buffett explaining why Berkshire sold its airline stocks in 2020, why he thinks Berkshire's large holdings might have changed the airlines' rescue by the government, and why Berkshire did not deploy its cash in March 2020 (no one can count on the Fed and Congress acting as they did). The upload date (2025) is not the event date. The clip is from the **2021** Berkshire annual meeting (high confidence). The clues: the questions refer to "the early months of Covid", the CARES Act and the March 2020 selloff as the past; Buffett describes the Fed's action on March 23rd and says that "right now" the economy is "running in super high gear" with "some inflation"; Munger is present and talking (he was absent from the 2020 meeting). The questions are read out by a moderator who is not named in the captions (at the 2021 meeting this was probably Becky Quick, but this is a guess). The "Chris Blaine" whose question is mentioned is kept as in the captions; he may be Chris Bloomstran, but that is a guess.
