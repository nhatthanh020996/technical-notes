# The Amazon-Berkshire-JPMorgan Health Care Venture, Keeping Political Views Out of Berkshire, and Managing Larger Sums

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DPAjw1E-fcI

Recorded: 2018 Berkshire annual meeting (high confidence)

> Note: The original YouTube title is "Warren Buffett: The Healthcare Industry Is A Total Scam". It is misleading: Buffett never calls health care a scam. He calls medical costs "a tapeworm" on American business's competitiveness and says every dollar in the system "has a constituency", and he explains the modest, non-profit-driven aims of the health care venture formed by three organizations (Berkshire, Amazon and JPMorgan, not named in the clip), which he expects to have a CEO "within a couple of months". The second half is a shareholder challenging his CNBC remarks on guns; Buffett says he will not impose his political views (he raised money for Hillary in 2016) on Berkshire's businesses. The clip is from the **2018** Berkshire annual meeting (high confidence). The clues: the venture was announced in January 2018 and its CEO was named in June 2018; Buffett's CNBC comments on guns followed the February 2018 Parkland shooting; health care is "close to 18%" of GDP and about $3.3 trillion. The last chapter ([10:03]) ends Munger's reply on guns and then appears to cut to a different answer about running "12 or 13 billion dollars" (probably the sums managed by Todd Combs and Ted Weschler; a guess).

## Part 3: Guns, tobacco, and not imposing political views on Berkshire's businesses ([07:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DPAjw1E-fcI&t=433))

**Questioner:** I was disturbed when you were asked on CNBC about the role that business could play in sensible policies around the sales of guns. You said you didn't think business should have a role at all and you wouldn't impose your values on others. I was even more surprised when you said you'd be okay with Berkshire owning shares in gun manufacturers. At this meeting years ago, you said you wouldn't buy a tobacco company because of the social issues. The idea that Berkshire would associate with any company as long as it isn't illegal seems at odds with everything I think you stand for. Please tell us you misspoke.

**Warren Buffett:** Well, let's explore that a little. Should it be just my view, or should it be the view of the owners of the company? So if I decide to poll the owners of the company on a variety of political issues, and one of them being whether, you know, Berkshire Hathaway should support the NRA [...] You know, I don't [...] If a majority of the shareholders voted to do it, or if a majority of the board of directors voted to do it, I wouldn't accept that.

I don't think that my political views [...] I don't think I put them in a blind trust at all when I take the job. In the election of 2016, I raised a lot of money, in my case I raised it for Hillary, and I spoke out in various ways that were quite frank. But I don't think, when I do that, I'm speaking for Berkshire. I'm speaking as a private citizen, and I don't think I have any business speaking for Berkshire.

We have never, at the parent company level, we have never made a political contribution. You know, I mean, I don't go to our suppliers, I don't do anything of that sort where I raise money, either for the school I went to or for a political candidate [unclear: "I went to"] or anything else. And I don't think that we should have a question on the GEICO policy [unclear: "hold reform"]: "Are you an NRA member?" You know, and if you are, you just aren't good enough for us, or something [...] I do not believe in imposing my political opinions on the activities of our businesses. And if you get to what companies are pure and which ones aren't pure, I think it is very difficult to make that call. But I think with that response, I'm almost afraid to call on Charlie, but go ahead.
