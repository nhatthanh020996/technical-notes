# Capital-Hungry Transportation Businesses, Choosing Role Models, Unpredictable Tech Economics, and the Hit-by-a-Truck Question

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c81WJLD-bGQ

Recorded: most likely 2000 Berkshire annual meeting (moderate confidence for Parts 2–4; low confidence for Part 1)

> Note: The original YouTube title is "Warren Buffett: Why You Must Never Buy Transportation Stocks". It is misleading: Buffett never says you must never buy transportation stocks. In the first 80 seconds he says transportation "has been a terrible place to have money" because railroads and airlines need huge capital investment for little return, and he adds that "there are plenty of exceptions". The rest of the clip is about role models, why he and Munger cannot predict the economics of tech companies 10 years out, what happens to Berkshire if he is hit by a truck, and the Omaha World-Herald. Parts 2–4 are most likely from the **2000** Berkshire annual meeting (moderate confidence). The clues: Buffett says Janet Lowe's biography of Charlie is "coming out ... later this year" (it was published in 2000); a questioner says he would have had "a cardiac arrest this last couple months", which fits the tech crash of spring 2000; Bob Noyce "died some years ago" (1990). The back-and-forth exchanges with one person asking several questions ("thank you for taking all three of these") look more like a press session or an informal Q&A than the main meeting, so the exact setting is uncertain. Part 1 (transportation) has no dating clues; it may be from a different meeting (low confidence).

## Part 1: Why transportation has been a terrible place to have money ([00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c81WJLD-bGQ&t=0))

*(The clip starts mid-answer; the question is not included.)*

**Warren Buffett:** Historically, the transportation field [...] I mean, it's been a terrible place to have money, whether it's been in airlines or in the rails. We've mentioned Value Line from here from time to time. If you go to the rail transportation section and just run your eye across on the revenues and look at the capital investment, the amount of capital required to produce incremental revenues is just horrible. And on the other hand, there's not much alternative here in the game to doing that. So many railroads will spend hundreds and hundreds and hundreds of millions of dollars, and it will not move the top line hardly at all. The ones where the top line has changed is where there's been acquisitions or mergers.

Airlines, you see just the opp[osite] [...] you see this great movement of the top line, but again, a disastrous amount of capital investment and very little in the way of returns. So it hasn't been a great field. Most fields that require heavy capital investment, most of the time they don't turn out very well over time. There are plenty of exceptions to that. But if you find a business that has to keep adding up huge sums of money every year, there'll always be a reason why they're doing it, but the net result after five or 10 or 20 years usually isn't very good.
