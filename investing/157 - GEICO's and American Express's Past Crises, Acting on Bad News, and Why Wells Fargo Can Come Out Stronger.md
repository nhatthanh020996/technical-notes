# GEICO's and American Express's Past Crises, Acting on Bad News, and Why Wells Fargo Can Come Out Stronger

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vhn6nCLvkFE

Recorded: 2018 Berkshire annual meeting (high confidence)

> Note: The original YouTube title is "Warren Buffett: Mistakes That Nearly Destroyed Billion Dollar Businesses". It is somewhat misleading: the mistakes discussed (GEICO's under-reserving in the 1970s and the American Express salad-oil / field-warehousing scandal of 1963) are told mainly to argue that companies that face up to such problems come out stronger, and the real subject is Wells Fargo's sales-practices scandal and why Buffett still likes it as an investment. The clip is from the **2018** Berkshire annual meeting (high confidence). The clues: Tim Sloan is Wells Fargo's current CEO, correcting "mistakes made by other people" (he was CEO from late 2016 to early 2019); Berkshire has 377,000 employees; Munger mentions Harvey Weinstein (the scandal broke in October 2017); and Buffett holds up the New York Times of March 12, 1942, which he brought to the 2018 meeting.

## Part 2: The American Express field warehousing scandal ([01:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vhn6nCLvkFE&t=69))

**Warren Buffett:** American Express was just picking up a few dollars by having the Field Warehousing Company in 1963. And they were worried whether it was going to sink the company, with some guy named Tino De Angelis in, I think it was, Bayonne, New Jersey.

In fact, I went to the annual meeting in 1964 of American Express after the scandal developed, and somebody asked if the auditor would step forward. And the auditor, from one of the big firms, which I won't mention, came up to the microphone, and somebody said, "How much did we pay you last year?" The auditor gave this answer, and then the questioner said, "Well, how much extra would you have charged us to go over to Bayonne, which was 10 miles away, and check whether there's any oil in the tanks?"

So here was something in a tiny little operation. Some guy was calling in from a bar in Bayonne and telling them funny stuff was going on, and they didn't want to hear it. They shut their ears to it. And then what emerged was one great company after this kind of, what they felt was, a near-death experience.

## Part 4: Two of the best investments came from such errors ([03:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vhn6nCLvkFE&t=204))

**Warren Buffett:** And the truth is, we've made a couple of our greatest investments where people have made similar errors. We bought our American Express [stock]; that was the best investment I ever made in my partnership years. We bought our American Express stock in 1964 because somebody was incented to do the wrong thing in something called the American Express Field Warehousing Company. We bought a very substantial amount of GEICO. We bought what became half of GEICO for $40 million because somebody was incented to meet Wall Street estimates of earnings and growth, and they didn't focus on having the proper reserves.

And that caused a lot of pain at American Express in 1964. It caused a lot of pain at GEICO in 1976. It caused a layoff of a significant portion of the workforce, all kinds of things. But they cleaned it up. They cleaned it up. And look where American Express has moved since that time. Look at where GEICO has moved since that time. So the fact that you are going to have problems at some very large institution is not unique. In fact, all the big banks have had troubles of one sort or another.
